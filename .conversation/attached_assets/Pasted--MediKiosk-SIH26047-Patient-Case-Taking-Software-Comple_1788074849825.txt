# 1. PROBLEM STATEMENT

The problem is about the clinical history-taking bottleneck in Indian hospitals.

Government hospital OPDs can have very high patient volumes, while doctors have very limited consultation time. During the consultation, doctors need to:

- collect chief complaint
- history of present illness
- past medical/surgical history
- drug/allergy history
- family history
- personal history
- review of systems
- review previous reports/prescriptions

Patients also bring physical medical documents such as:

- prescriptions
- laboratory reports
- discharge summaries
- imaging reports

These are often unstructured, handwritten, in different languages, and chronologically disordered.

The proposed solution is a patient-facing clinical intake platform that collects history **before the doctor consultation** and presents a structured summary to the doctor.

The original problem statement proposes:

- multilingual voice interaction
- touchscreen interaction
- adaptive clinical questioning
- medical document digitization/OCR
- structured clinical summary
- red-flag detection
- ABDM/ABHA integration
- hospital HIS integration
- privacy/consent
- AYUSH history mode

However, **we are NOT implementing all of these in the prototype.**

# 33. CROSS-QUESTIONING — VERY IMPORTANT

The prototype must be designed so the team can defend it.

## Q: Why do we need this?

Answer:

> “Existing hospital registration systems primarily collect administrative information. MediKiosk focuses on the clinical history-taking bottleneck and prepares a structured history before consultation.”

---

## Q: Why not just use a mobile app?

Answer:

> “A mobile-only solution excludes some elderly, low-literacy and first-time users. MediKiosk provides an assisted entry point while MediWeb/mobile can serve digitally comfortable patients.”

---

## Q: Why both kiosk and web?

Answer:

> “They solve different accessibility needs while using the same clinical workflow and producing the same structured output for the doctor.”

---

## Q: Why would an illiterate patient use the kiosk?

Answer:

> “The kiosk minimizes reading and typing. It uses audio guidance, large visual choices, Hindi/English support and voice/touch interaction.”

---

## Q: Is this diagnosing the patient?

Answer:

> “No. MediKiosk is a clinical history acquisition and summarization system. Priority alerts identify predefined red-flag combinations, but diagnosis and clinical decisions remain with the doctor.”

---

## Q: What happens if AI makes a mistake?

Answer:

> “The generated summary is treated as a draft. The doctor can review, edit and confirm it before it becomes part of the clinical record.”

---

## Q: What if the patient gives wrong information?

Answer:

> “The system doesn't independently verify patient statements. That's why information remains attributable to its source and the doctor validates it.”

---

## Q: How does adaptive questioning work?

Answer:

> “The prototype uses complaint-specific clinical pathways. For example, chest pain leads to questions about onset, severity, radiation and associated symptoms. In production, this can be expanded using a clinical dialogue engine.”

---

## Q: How is this AI?

Answer:

> “The current prototype demonstrates the workflow using predefined clinical pathways and rules. Production AI components would handle multilingual speech recognition, natural-language understanding, document extraction and summarization.”

---

## Q: Why only three diseases?

Answer:

> “We selected three representative pathways for the prototype. Chest pain demonstrates red-flag screening, abdominal pain demonstrates symptom-based adaptive questioning, and diabetes demonstrates chronic disease history and previous records. The architecture can be extended to more pathways.”

---

## Q: What about ABHA?

Answer:

> “ABHA/ABDM integration is part of the production architecture but isn't implemented in this frontend prototype.”

---

## Q: Why no backend?

Answer:

> “The current prototype focuses on validating the patient-to-doctor workflow. Backend persistence and integration are the next implementation layer.”

---

## Q: What about privacy?

Answer:

> “Health information requires strong privacy controls. The production system would use authentication, encryption, consent-based sharing, controlled access and secure handling of temporary kiosk sessions.”

---

## Q: What if internet goes down?

Future answer:

> “A production kiosk should support an offline-first fallback for basic history collection and synchronize securely once connectivity is restored.”

---

## Q: What if the hospital is noisy?

Answer:

> “Voice should not be the only input. Touch remains available as a fallback. Production deployment can use noise-robust speech recognition and appropriate microphones.”

---

## Q: What if the patient can't speak?

Answer:

> “Voice is optional. Every question can also be answered through touch.”

---

## Q: Why not use nurses?

Answer:

> “MediKiosk is not intended to replace nurses. It handles repetitive structured intake so clinical staff can focus on cases requiring human assistance and clinical judgment.”

---

## Q: What happens if OCR reads a medicine incorrectly?

Answer:

> “Extracted information remains unverified. The production interface should show the source document and allow the doctor to correct extracted information before confirmation.”

---

## Q: Can it work for AYUSH?

Answer:

> “Yes. The question engine can support configurable clinical pathways. An AYUSH pathway can later capture Dashavidha Pariksha and Ahara-Vihara information.”

But do NOT implement full AYUSH right now.
